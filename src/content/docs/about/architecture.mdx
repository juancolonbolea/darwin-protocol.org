## What the protocol does — and what it does not

The DARWIN Protocol has one job: **mint trust**. Everything that requires a balance sheet is handled by partners. Everything that mints trust is the protocol.

This distinction is the foundation of the architecture.

---

## The protocol provides

| Component | Function |
|-----------|----------|
| **Risk engine** | Logs every trade, normalises to standard risk, produces the Quote |
| **Matching engine** | Pools flow, nets internally, overflows to market |
| **Dataset** | 12+ years of verified track records — the compound learning curve |
| **Community** | Leaderboard, marketplace, diagnostics, peer benchmarks |
| **INDX** | The collective performance metric — the proof |

These components are the core. They cannot be extracted, replicated, or captured by any single entity. They belong to the economy.

---

## Partners provide everything else

| Partner | Function |
|---------|----------|
| **Member brokers** | Market access, execution, liquidity |
| **Clearing partners** | Settlement, custody, regulatory compliance |
| **Portfolio managers** | Distribution, investor relations, fund structure |
| **Technology partners** | Infrastructure, connectivity, data services |

The protocol never holds your money. It never brokers a trade. It never custodies an asset. It never extends credit. It never takes the other side of a position.

This is not a limitation. It is the design.

---

## The Visa analogy

Consider how Visa works. Visa does not extend credit. Visa does not issue cards. Visa does not hold deposits. Visa connects parties — cardholders, merchants, banks — through a trusted network protocol.

The DARWIN Protocol works the same way:

| Visa | DARWIN Protocol |
|------|-----------------|
| Connects cardholders to merchants | Connects traders to investors |
| Does not hold money | Does not hold money |
| Does not extend credit | Does not broker trades |
| Network trust enables transactions | Verified track records enable allocation |
| Interchange fee funds the network | Trust-flow split funds the economy |

The protocol is the trust layer. Partners provide everything above and below it.

---

## Why this matters

### No single point of failure

Because the protocol does not hold money, custody assets, or broker trades, there is no central balance sheet that can fail. The risk engine, dataset, and matching engine can operate independently of any single partner. If a broker fails, traders move to another broker on the same rails. The track records survive.

### No value capture

The trust — the verified track records, the dataset, the community's collective credibility — stays with the members. No company can acquire the protocol and extract the trust for its own benefit. The architecture prevents it.

### Regulatory clarity

By separating trust-minting (the protocol) from money-handling (partners), each component operates within clear regulatory boundaries. Brokers are regulated as brokers. Portfolio managers are regulated as managers. The protocol handles what it handles: data, verification, and community.

---

## The result

A system where:

- **Members** own their track records permanently
- **Investors** access verified performance without counterparty risk to the protocol
- **Partners** provide regulated services within their expertise
- **The protocol** mints trust and reinvests surplus into the community

Built to last means built so that no single entity — including the founding team — can capture the value the community creates. The trust belongs to the members who earned it.
