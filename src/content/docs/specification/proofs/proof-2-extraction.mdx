## Claim

**T20 — Three extraction levels must all be closed.** Corruption-resistance requires closing extraction at the flow level, the stock level, and the governance level simultaneously. Closing any one or two of the three leaves the protocol exploitable.

## Premises

- **A9 (no counterparty extraction)** — no party, the operator included, may take the other side of member flow or trade on aggregate flow information. This closes the *flow* level. The mechanics that implement it inside the venue are a conformance requirement on the matching and settlement components, not axiom content.
- **T19 (deadwood shareholders are stock-level extraction)** — a shareholder who holds ownership without ongoing contribution extracts at the *stock* level, in violation of P6 (merit).
- **T12 (power inversion organises individuals off the menu)** — governance power that is not itself constrained can block reinvestment even when flow and stock are clean.

## Derivation

1. **A9** closes the flow level: no party may take the other side of member flow, so no intermediary can skim the matching engine's spread. It is silent on what happens to accumulated ownership.
2. **T19** shows that even with flow closed, a zero-contribution shareholder still extracts — value flows to holders who no longer add trust, violating P6.
3. Even with flow and stock both closed, governance power can still block reinvestment decisions, redirecting surplus away from the vectors that would mint the most trust (T17).
4. Each channel is independent: closing one does not close, or even constrain, the others. A protocol clean at the flow level can still be captured at the stock or governance level.
5. Therefore corruption-resistance requires all three constitutional simultaneously — flow (A9), stock (T19 resolved), and governance (T12 resolved). $\blacksquare$

## Load-bearing assumptions

- **The three levels are genuinely independent.** The proof's step 4 is doing the real work — if closing the flow level structurally implied closing the stock or governance level, this would collapse to a simpler claim. It does not: a prohibition on taking the other side of member flow (A9) says nothing about who owns equity or who controls the reinvestment decision.
- **T19 and T12 hold on their own terms.** T19 depends on A9 + T15 (front-loading) + A7. T12 depends on A4 + A9 + A14 (power inversion).

## What breaks if a premise fails

If **A9 fails**, the venue itself becomes an intermediary — surplus leaks out at the flow level regardless of what happens to stock or governance. This is failure theorem **F4 (★-Collapse)**: the pre-transition broker state.

If **T19's stock-level closure fails** (ownership is not tied to contribution), the "landed gentry" failure mode reappears: early contributors accumulate stock, stop contributing, and extract indefinitely even while flow stays clean. T61 (DWX Decay) is the constitution's structural answer — ownership decays without sustained contribution.

If **T12's governance closure fails**, a clean flow-and-stock system can still be steered to under-fund the highest-trust-minting vector, quietly reintroducing extraction through allocation decisions rather than through the ledger.
