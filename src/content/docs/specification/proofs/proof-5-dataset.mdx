## Claim

**T6 — The dataset is the trust→edge vehicle.** A7 asserts that trust and edge reinforce one another but does not specify how. T6 identifies the mechanism: the accumulated dataset (P5), which serves a dual function as the α-vehicle (converting trust into edge across five channels) and as the risk engine's training corpus (converting trust into more precise standardisation).

## Premises

- **A7 (β, axiom)** — trust attracts confidence: outsiders observe collective proof and allocate capital. The α leg — trust improving edge — is the theorem T56, and the double recurrence is what motivates asking *how* trust becomes edge.
- **P5 (the dataset)** — twelve years of certified track records, matching patterns, and risk-adjusted histories; non-portable and protocol-owned.
- **A8 (commensurability)** — per-agent estimates admit a common ordering across heterogeneous agents, which is what the training-corpus half of T6's claim sharpens.

## Derivation

1. The double recurrence asserts a trust → edge relationship but, taken alone, is silent on mechanism — it states the channel exists, not what carries it.
2. Five channels carry that relationship in practice: inheritance (new members draw on accumulated patterns), matching intelligence, the risk engine itself, the variometer, and community knowledge-sharing.
3. Each channel converts accumulated trust — recorded, attributed data — into improved edge for participants, present or future.
4. Without P5, A7's trust → confidence leg could still hold (capital can follow proof without a growing dataset), but the trust → edge leg has no substrate to act through. $e$ would be constant, and $e^X$ collapses to $e \times X$ — linear, not exponential.
5. Therefore the dataset is not incidental to the trust → edge claim; it *is* the vehicle. $\blacksquare$

## Load-bearing assumptions

- **The five channels are genuinely distinct and additive.** If they collapsed into one mechanism, T6's "dual function" framing (α-vehicle and training corpus) would be doing less work than claimed.
- **P5 keeps growing.** T6 depends on the dataset accumulating over time — a static or shrinking dataset cannot serve as an ongoing vehicle.
- **A8 (commensurability)** is presupposed for the training-corpus half of T6's claim: richer data sharpens the risk engine's calibration only if its estimates land on a single ordering that holds across heterogeneous agents. How that ordering is computed, and how deterministic the computation is, are governance parameters rather than axiom content.

## What breaks if a premise fails

If **P5 stops growing or degrades** (contamination, staleness, or a pipeline failure), the α-vehicle fails. This is failure theorem **F1 (α-Collapse)**: $e$ decouples from $X$, and growth becomes additive rather than compounding. Adversarially, this is also the failure surface named in **F6 (Dataset Poisoning)** — a coordinated coalition submitting crafted signals to bias the aggregate statistics the risk engine learns from.

If **A7 fails** — trust stops attracting confidence — the training-corpus half of T6 can still hold — the risk engine keeps improving — but nobody is funding the edge that improvement produces. That is **F2 (β-Collapse)**, not a failure of T6 itself.
