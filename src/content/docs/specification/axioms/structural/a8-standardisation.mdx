## Statement

Per-agent estimates admit a common ordering across heterogeneous agents. Not implied by validity.

## v13 note

The statement is now the cross-agent claim alone: estimates of individual contribution can be placed on one ordering across heterogeneous agents, which validity (A2) does not imply. The three structural features that used to sit inside the statement move out — the CCP arrangement and arm's-length independence become a corollary of A9 plus P6, and algorithmic determinism becomes a governance parameter of the risk engine rather than an axiom clause. The restatement also requires stripping the enabling clause ("without comparability, K = 0") from Confidence (spec P2), which was making A8 analytic. Source: the axiom stress test, `analysis/axiom-stress/SYNTHESIS.md` in the repository.

## Grounded in

P1 (Credibility) + P2 (Confidence) + A2. P1 and P2 are the two sides A8 orders between; A2 supplies the per-agent estimates the common ordering is defined over. Commensurability across agents is not implied by validity for one agent — that gap is the whole of the claim.

## Circle

e→X membrane — A8 is not a circle but the translator between the credibility circle and the confidence circle.

## Why it is an axiom

A8 was demoted once (to a theorem, T5, in an earlier version) and then restored with different content — which is itself evidence of the independence check working as designed. The restored claim is not that $e^X$ is algebraically literal (that later became a separate theorem) but that *commensurability has to be posited*: capital cannot rationally allocate across an idiosyncratic, incomparable set of signals, however well each one is estimated. This is not derivable from A2 — A2 establishes that one agent's contribution can be estimated; A8 makes the separate claim that those estimates admit a common ordering across heterogeneous agents. Nothing about validity for one agent implies commensurability across many.

## What it explains

A8 grounds T6 (the dataset serves both the edge loop and the risk engine's training corpus), T8 (three panels feed X, presupposing A8's comparability), T53 (Market Existence), and T54 (Two-Step Resolution). It is the explicit precondition of A7's β-loop — the Constitution states $\beta = 0$ without A8 — and couples with the α-loop: richer data improves risk-engine calibration, which sharpens standardisation, which strengthens confidence.

## Force mapping

A8 powers **S (Selection)**, alongside A4 and A14. The Constitution separately describes A8 as **the membrane** between the internal forces (V/S/I) and the external force (P): without standardisation, credibility cannot cross the boundary to attract confidence, and $K = 0$.

## Economic precedent

A8 directly satisfies **Holmström's informativeness principle** (1982): the risk engine includes every signal statistically informative about trader quality, which is why a single Quote is not a sufficient statistic and multi-dimensional scoring is theoretically optimal rather than incidental complexity. A8, jointly with A9, approximates Holmström's first-best allocation by making trader actions fully observable (T54). The Constitution also names Holmström's **career-concerns** effect: a published, continuously-updating track record creates implicit incentive beyond the explicit fee structure.
