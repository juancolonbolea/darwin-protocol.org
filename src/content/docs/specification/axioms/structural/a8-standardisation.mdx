## Statement

Credibility (P1) can only attract confidence (P2) when expressed as a comparable instrument, manufactured by a trusted, independent process. Read as an information translator: A8 converts quality information — raw edge, idiosyncratic, incomparable — into allocation information: comparable, investable, rational. Without A8, quality information exists but cannot be acted on. Confidence is zero identically.

Three structural features make this work:

1. **The CCP faces investors.** Darwinex is the centralised counterparty — the investor trusts the institution, not the trader. This is a trust architecture, not a marketing claim.
2. **Arm's length from providers.** The risk engine operates independently of the signal provider. A trader cannot game the manufacturing of their own instrument.
3. **Algorithmic, not discretionary.** A deterministic process — normalisation, engine-generated trades at fixed intervals, VaR scaling to a fixed benchmark. Every adjustment is auditable; no human judgment sits in the loop.

Together these three features build the comparison surface: any certified instrument can be evaluated against any other. A8's credibility rests on [A9](/specification/axioms/structural/a9-no-extraction/) — if the venue's integrity fails, the standardiser is biased, A8 loses trust, and the β-loop collapses to zero.

## Grounded in

P1 (Credibility) + P2 (Confidence) + P4 (Matching) + A9 (No Extraction). A8 sits between P1 and P2 as the membrane that makes the transition between them rational, and depends on A9 for its own integrity.

## Circle

e→X membrane — A8 is not a circle but the translator between the credibility circle and the confidence circle.

## Why it is an axiom

A8 was demoted once (to a theorem, T5, in an earlier version) and then restored with different content — which is itself evidence of the independence check working as designed. The restored claim is not that $e^X$ is algebraically literal (that later became a separate theorem) but that *investability itself has a precondition*: capital cannot rationally allocate to an idiosyncratic, incomparable signal, no matter how well-certified. This is not derivable from A2 (merit can be certified) — A2 establishes that quality can be measured; A8 makes the separate claim that measurement alone is insufficient, and that a further translation step — standardisation into a common, algorithmically-produced comparison surface — is required before confidence can rationally form. Nothing about certification implies comparability.

## What it explains

A8 grounds T6 (the dataset serves both the edge loop and the risk engine's training corpus), T8 (three panels feed X, presupposing A8's comparability), T53 (Market Existence), and T54 (Two-Step Resolution). It is the explicit precondition of A7's β-loop — the Constitution states $\beta = 0$ without A8 — and couples with the α-loop: richer data improves risk-engine calibration, which sharpens standardisation, which strengthens confidence.

## Force mapping

A8 powers **S (Selection)**, alongside A4, A9, and A14. The Constitution separately describes A8 as **the membrane** between the internal forces (V/S/I) and the external force (P): without standardisation, credibility cannot cross the boundary to attract confidence, and $K = 0$.

## Economic precedent

A8 directly satisfies **Holmström's informativeness principle** (1982): the risk engine includes every signal statistically informative about trader quality, which is why a single Quote is not a sufficient statistic and multi-dimensional scoring is theoretically optimal rather than incidental complexity. A8, jointly with A9, approximates Holmström's first-best allocation by making trader actions fully observable (T54). The Constitution also names Holmström's **career-concerns** effect: a published, continuously-updating track record creates implicit incentive beyond the explicit fee structure.
