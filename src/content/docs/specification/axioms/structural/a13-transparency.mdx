## Statement

Results are public. IP is encrypted. Process stays proprietary. Individual selfishness converts into social trust.

A13 is the credibility inversion rendered as a protocol rule — the generative method applied to credibility asymmetry: convention (performance hidden) → inversion (results public, certified, comparable) → test (does trust emerge? yes — adverse selection resolves). A13 is how the protocol inverts the credibility dimension of P0.

## Grounded in

P1 (Credibility) + P6 (Merit). P1 is what gets published; P6 is why publication rather than concealment is the correct rule.

## Circle

e mechanism — A13 is the operating rule of the credibility circle, not a separate circle.

## Why it is an axiom

A13 asserts a specific, non-obvious design choice — publish results while encrypting the method that produced them — and claims this specific split resolves adverse selection. That claim does not follow from A2 (merit can be certified) alone: A2 establishes that certification is possible, but says nothing about what must be made public versus kept proprietary, or why that particular split is the one that converts individual selfishness into social trust rather than, say, full disclosure or full opacity. A13 is a distinct structural commitment about *which* information the inversion exposes.

## What it explains

A13 is one of five grounding axioms for T1 (self-accelerating flywheel) and directly grounds T11 (transparency turns selfishness into social trust, with A2+A7), T27 (Protocol Network Corollary), T28 (Linear-Network Decomposition), T30 (open-source protection), T31 (certification externality), T33 (nested meritocracy), T34 (trust ownership), T42 (external validation), T47/T56 (aggregate α derivation), and T49 (star factory). It is also a grounding input to T53 (Market Existence) and T54 (Two-Step Resolution).

## Force mapping

A13 powers **V (Variation)**, alongside A1, A2, and A3 — publication is what makes newly-produced edge visible enough to be selected on. Without it: F5 (Stagnation).

## Economic precedent

A13 directly resolves **Akerlof's adverse selection** (2001): the DARWIN Quote is a *direct* quality signal, not a proxy like education in Spence's model, which eliminates market-for-lemons dynamics outright rather than merely mitigating them. It is named explicitly in the Constitution as the mechanism that resolves this.
