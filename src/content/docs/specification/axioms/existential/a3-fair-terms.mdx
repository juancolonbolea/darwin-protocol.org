## Statement

One contract, offered to every prospective provider. Entry is not gated by negotiated terms, discretionary underwriting, capital minimum or prior standing, and terms are not indexed to certified quality. Uniformity constrains the venue; it asserts nothing about who enters.

## v13 note

The statement now constrains the venue rather than predicting the population. The screening claim — that uniform terms produce a separating equilibrium — is demoted into T53, and the "totality" of self-selection is dropped, because it was a prediction about who enters rather than a rule about what is offered. The entry-cost vector (time, fee, drawdown, publication) becomes a governance parameter, bounded below by fraud deterrence and above by variation supply. Source: the axiom stress test, `analysis/axiom-stress/SYNTHESIS.md` in the repository.

## Grounded in

P6 (Merit) + A1. P6 is why uniformity, rather than negotiated or tiered terms, is the correct design. A1 is the condition uniformity answers: access-gated entry is precisely the mechanism by which access substitutes for contribution.

## Circle

The e edge — A3 is the gate at the boundary of the credibility circle, deciding who gets to start accumulating a Quote at all.

## Why it is an axiom

A3 could look derivable from A1+A2 — "if merit can be certified, just offer everyone the same terms and let certification sort them out." But that skips the actual claim: A3 constrains what the venue may offer, prior to and independent of any certification event. A system could certify merit (A2) and still gate entry by negotiated terms, discretionary underwriting, capital minimums, or terms indexed to certified quality. A3 rules all of that out. It is not implied by A1 or A2; it is a separate design axiom about how access itself is structured.

## What it explains

A3 grounds T53 (Market Existence — Akerlof/Spence), which now carries the screening argument itself, alongside P0, P1, A2, and A13, and feeds T42/T47 through A13's dependence chain. It is also one of the axioms F5 (Stagnation) names directly: if A3 is "over-optimised" — barriers raised until self-selection excludes all but incumbents — variation dries up and A1 (access, not merit) reasserts itself inside the protocol.

## Force mapping

A3 powers **V (Variation)**, alongside A1, A2, and A13 — it is the condition under which new edge can enter the pool at all. Without it, entry is discretionary rather than merit-screened, and the system risks F5.

## Economic precedent

A3 is a direct instance of **Rothschild-Stiglitz screening** (1976): a single contract offered universally, engineered so that self-selection under it reveals type without the principal observing type directly. The four cost dimensions additionally satisfy **Spence's** (1973) single-crossing condition, which is what guarantees the separating equilibrium holds — skilled traders in, tourists out — rather than collapsing to a pooling equilibrium.
