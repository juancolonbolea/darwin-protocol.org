T1–T28 establish the flywheel, the exponential, the two inversions, the S-curve, and extraction resistance. Everything from here extends those results into the protocol's transition path, its certification architecture, its relationship to prior economic theory, and its endgame monetary design. Entries are compact and grouped by the topic headings the constitution itself uses. Numbering runs T29–T64; T62 and T63 joined the active set at v14, when A17 was adopted.

## Moat, Openness & Network Effects (T29–T34)

**T29 — Moat Dimensionality.** Moat strength is the product of three participant types (signal, infrastructure, capital) across five matching dimensions — fifteen vectors total. A competitor missing any one vector gets zero replication, not partial replication, because an unpopulated dimension contributes a zero factor to a multiplicative product ([L1](/specification/theorems/core-flywheel)). **Derives from** A4+L1+T2+T7+T28. **Consequence:** partial replication doesn't approximate the moat, it collapses it — the structure is multiplicative, not additive. *Proof 7 in the constitution.*

**T30 — Open-Source Protection.** Open-sourcing the protocol's methods strengthens the dataset moat rather than weakening it, because methods are platform-specific while the dataset ($P5$), not the method, is the actual trust→edge vehicle (T6). **Derives from** T6+A13+P5. **Consequence:** openness is a growth lever, not a risk — a fork of the method without the dataset is structurally inert. *Proof 8 in the constitution.*

**T31 — Certification Externality.** Each new certified member badge increases the value of every existing badge — A13 applied at the node level. **Derives from** A2+A7+A13+T27. **Consequence:** certification value compounds with adoption, giving certification the dynamics of a network good.

**T32 — Regulatory Architecture (normative design theorem).** The architecture should concentrate regulatory obligations where capital actually flows and structurally separate every other matching dimension as unregulated periphery, minimizing compliance surface while letting non-capital dimensions grow unconstrained. **Derives from** P4+A9+A2+P6. **Consequence:** if a peripheral activity's scale or risk later requires regulation, structural separation lets it move into the regulated core cleanly, without redesigning the rest of the system.

**T33 — Nested Meritocracy.** The token hierarchy — DARWIN, Community, INDX, DWX — applies selection at every level; communities that outperform the aggregate get absorbed into it, and regulatory scope escalates with aggregation level. Selection can recur at each level only because estimates are commensurable across heterogeneous agents (A8) — otherwise the levels cannot be compared with one another. **Derives from** A2+A7+A8+T11+T27+P6. **Consequence:** meritocratic selection is not a single filter applied once — it recurs at every layer of aggregation.

**T34 — Trust Ownership.** Trust minted by the protocol is protocol property, not individual property: edge ($P1$) is portable when a member leaves, trust ($P3$) is not — it remains inside the dataset ($P5$). **Derives from** P3+P5+A9+A13. **Consequence:** switching cost is asymmetric — leaving forfeits accumulated trust while joining costs nothing, which makes the protocol a trust gravity well rather than a marketplace members can costlessly re-enter.

## Protocol Transition & Endgame (T35–T41)

**T35 — Stock/Flow Duality.** Protocol value exists simultaneously as a participation flow (INDX = $e \times X$, earned per period) and an ownership stock (DWX = $e^X$, the NPV of all future flows); the ratio DWX/INDX is the growth premium on ownership. The primitive types carry through to the token layer: credibility ($P1$) is a stock with rate $dC/dt$, confidence ($P2$) is a flow into the assets-under-management stock, and trust ($P3$) is the stock $T = C \times K$. **Trust is the stock; INDX is the flow off it per period**, and the rate at which retained surplus compounds on that stock is $e$, taken from A5's Compound vector. **Derives from** P1+P2+P3+A5+T5+T9. **Consequence:** "how much INDX did I earn" and "what is my DWX worth" answer two related but distinct questions — flow now versus a claim on all future flow. *Proof 9 in the constitution.*

**T36 — Ownership S-Curve (DWX:INDX Convergence).** The DWX:INDX exchange rate declines from infinity at founding to 1 at maturity, tracing the inverse of the adoption S-curve (T16). **Derives from** T35+T16+T3. **Consequence:** "returning markets to members" has a precise definition here — the statement DWX:INDX → 1, not a slogan. *Proof 10 in the constitution.*

**T37 — Self-Reinforcing Ownership Transfer.** Converting earned INDX into DWX ownership is positive-sum: more alignment produces more edge contribution, which produces more INDX generation, which raises DWX value for everyone, including holders who did not convert. **Derives from** T35+A5+A7+T1. **Consequence:** ownership transfer to members does not dilute existing holders — it is the mechanism by which the whole protocol becomes more valuable. *Proof 11 in the constitution.*

**T38 — Progressive Shedding.** The transition from a company structure ($e \times X$) to a protocol structure ($e^X$) requires progressively externalizing balance-sheet-heavy activities to partners for whom each activity is core business, while the center retains method, permission, sovereign capital, and rail economics. **Derives from** T27+T24+T28+A5. **Consequence:** each externalized activity adds flow to the matching engine and raises the internal matching rate for everyone — shedding is how the transition is executed, not downsizing.

**T39 — Endgame Entity (capstone).** The steady-state entity is a member-owned prop fund that owns a trust-minting protocol: no client money, no balance-sheet-heavy operations, sovereign capital only, revenue from rail rental, permission fees, and netting surplus. **Derives from** T36+T38+T34+P6+T3. **Consequence:** the founding team's exit and the mission's completion are the same event — both occur exactly when DWX:INDX → 1. *Proof 12 in the constitution.*

**T40 — Shareholder Exit Mechanism.** The optimal exit window is during the $e^X$ phase, while the DWX:INDX premium is declining but still substantial; members buy DWX with earned INDX, so the INDX:DWX market is itself the exit — no external liquidity event is required. **Derives from** T36+T15+T37+T19. **Consequence:** front-loading (T15) is repaid through DWX appreciation, and deadwood extraction (T19) resolves automatically as convergence removes any premium left to extract.

**T41 — Challenge Marketplace.** Structured challenges are member-settled peer-to-peer contests where the protocol supplies only the venue, the rules, and the Quote as judge; the community pool market-makes the challenges, and value returns to members minus a mediation fee. **Derives from** A2+A4+A9+T34+P6. **Consequence:** this closes the last extraction level (T20) at the product layer — revenue is rail revenue, not product margin, and every challenge feeds the certification loop it depends on. *Proof 13 in the constitution.*

## Certification & Attribution (T42–T47)

**T42 — External Validation.** Internal certification (the Quote) becomes credible only when tested against an external market: overflow that beats the external benchmark is the certification event itself. The oracle is bilateral — the external market certifies both minting (positive) and destruction (negative) of trust stock, posted as a double-entry ledger. **Derives from** A2+A4+A7+A13. **Consequence:** T42 is the framework's falsifiability condition — every downstream structural claim, including $e^X$ itself, ultimately rests on overflow continuing to beat the external market. *Proof 14 in the constitution.*

**T43 — Two-Way Certification.** The protocol certifies in both directions at once: to capital, "this edge is proven"; to edge, "this protocol enforces fair measurement and reward." Both certifications run on one mechanism — a single estimator of contribution (A2) applied under a single ordering principle (P6), read once toward capital and once toward edge. Independent measurement, public attestation and external validation are that one mechanism seen from either side, not three separate guarantees. **Derives from** A2+A14+A3+P6+T34. **Consequence:** the flywheel needs both directions running simultaneously — without edge→capital certification no allocation enters, and without protocol→edge certification no contribution enters. *Proof 15 in the constitution.*

**T44 — Value Chain Certification.** The protocol certifies contribution at every step of the value chain — sourcing, developing, processing, compounding — so that INDX functions as attributed certification of what a member did, not a reward for what they hold. Per-cycle attribution is well defined because the record is permanent ($P5$) and each cycle's postings commit atomically — the latter a conformance requirement on the matching and settlement components, not an axiom. **Derives from** A2+A4+P5+P6+T43. **Consequence:** this is what makes INDX a utility instrument rather than a security — it certifies documented activity, traceable across the full lifecycle. *Proof 16 in the constitution.*

**T45 — The Bidirectional Conduit.** The funnel connecting prospects to owners is not one-directional; it is a matching surface where personal merit flows in and social reward flows back at every step, applying A4 to the value chain itself. **Derives from** A4+A7+T2+T3+P6+T44. **Consequence:** at civilizational scale, this is the mechanism behind "returning markets to people" — T3 and T45 operating together, not a metaphor layered on top of the mechanics.

**T46 — The Waterfall (Asset Class Cascade).** Growth proceeds recursively by asset class: achieve critical internal matching in one class, demonstrate statistically significant edge, then redeploy the resulting surplus to attack the next class. Cross-asset netting means each new class raises the internal matching rate for every existing class simultaneously. **Derives from** T2+T3+T16+T21+A5+T45. **Consequence:** adding an asset class is not merely additive expansion — it multiplicatively strengthens every class already inside the protocol.

**T47 — Network Credibility Theorem (G16 resolution).** Aggregate α — trust improving edge across the whole population — is derivable rather than assumed, via two channels: a network channel (trust → confidence → flow → richer dataset → better selection) and a marketplace channel (trust makes internal transactions individually rational, raising measurement density and selection quality). Individual-level improvement stays empirical; it is not required for the aggregate result. **Derives from** A7+A4+A8+P5+T18+A13+T11+T34+A2. **Consequence:** the exponential claim (T5) does not depend on any single member getting better — it depends on the population's selection mechanism improving, a structural claim rather than a psychological one.

## Organizational Extraction (T48)

**T48 — Organizational Extraction Test.** Any organizational layer — business unit, role, partner, process, feature — that net-consumes trust stock without minting or protecting it at any level is extraction. The test: does removing the layer raise, lower, or hold constant the rate of trust minting? Layers that protect trust without directly minting it (compliance, risk management) are not extraction — removing them would raise the risk of venue failure (F4). **Derives from** A5+T20+P6. **Consequence:** T48 gives every hiring decision, partnership, and product feature a single test — mint, protect, or extract — rather than requiring a bespoke justification each time.

## Information Architecture (T49–T52)

**T49 — Star Factory.** The exchange amplifies individual success into collective acquisition: a member's public, certified track record lowers acquisition costs for everyone else, and because trust ownership is non-portable (T34), a departing star cannot take their certified history with them. **Derives from** A2+A13+T31+T34+P1. **Consequence:** the mechanism is structurally stronger than a conventional talent market, because the asset that makes a star valuable to the platform stays with the platform.

**T50 — Information Lifecycle.** Information passes through three stages mapped to the three circles: CREATE at the individual level (certify edge), PROTECT at the community level (mutualized matching hides intent), LIBERATE at the societal level (open dataset). The order is necessary — uncertified information cannot be protected, and unprotected information cannot be liberated. **Derives from** P0+P1+P4+P5+T45. **Consequence:** each resolving primitive ($P1$, $P4$, $P5$) maps to exactly one stage of this lifecycle and one dimension of information asymmetry ($P0$).

**T51 — Non-Rivalry Duality.** Information is non-rivalrous inside the community (one member's edge does not diminish another's; $N$ members produce $O(N^2)$ matching pairs) but rivalrous against the outside (aggregate edge erodes external market-makers' advantage). This dual rivalry is the physical mechanism behind $e^X$. **Derives from** P0+A4+A9+T5+T2. **Consequence:** A9 is what preserves this asymmetry — if internal exploitation were allowed, information would become rivalrous inside too, and growth would fall back to linear.

**T52 — Trust as Residual.** Trust is not built; it is revealed by removing information asymmetry: $T = T_{max} - A$, where $A$ decomposes into credibility, intent, and data asymmetry, and $dT/dt = -dA/dt$. **Derives from** P0+P1+P4+P5+P3. **Consequence:** this inverts the conventional model of trust-building — the protocol does not add reputation on top of a relationship, it removes the barriers preventing trust from being the default equilibrium.

## Nobel Cross-Check (T53–T55)

**T53 — Market Existence (Akerlof–Spence).** Without certification ($P1$), the market for edge allocation fails to exist by Akerlof's unraveling argument: unobservable quality collapses to an average price, skilled traders withdraw, quality falls further, and only tourists remain. The Quote satisfies Spence's single-crossing condition — signal cost strictly decreases in trader quality — guaranteeing a separating equilibrium. **Derives from** P0+P1+A2+A3+A8+A13. **Consequence:** certification is not an efficiency improvement on an existing market — without it there is no market to improve; the alternative is not a worse market but no market at all.

**T54 — Two-Step Resolution (Holmström → Akerlof).** The system resolves both canonical agency problems in sequence: a valid per-agent estimator (A2) placed on a common ordering (A8), plus the prohibition that keeps member flow out of any counterparty's book (A9), make trader actions observable — converting moral hazard into a bounded adverse-selection problem, which certification ($P1$) then resolves. **Derives from** A2+A8+A9+P1+A13+T53. **Consequence:** the matching engine converts "the trader might cheat" into "the trader's performance is measured and priced" — a solvable problem rather than an unresolvable trust gap.

**T55 — Provision Commons (Ostrom).** The dataset is a provision commons, not an appropriation commons — the governance challenge is sustaining contribution, not restricting extraction of a depletable resource, because information is non-rivalrous (T51). The system scores against Ostrom's commons design principles. **Derives from** P5+A9+A4+P6+T51. **Consequence:** sustainability requires that the return to contributing exceed the cost of contributing for a critical mass of skilled participants — a measurable design target, not an aspiration.

## Axiom Demotion & Interface (T56–T57)

**T56 — Trust-to-Edge Aggregation.** The aggregate α-loop is derivable rather than axiomatic — T47's channel specialised to the edge dimension. Individual-level improvement remains empirical; aggregate improvement is proven. **Derives from** A7+A4+A8+P5+T18+A13+T11+T34+A2. **Consequence:** the framework needs only one behavioural axiom (A7, the β-loop) to generate exponential growth, not two. *Proof 17 in the constitution.*

**T57 — Open Trust ≡ V/S/I/P Operational Interface.** The four Open Trust disclosure axes — Data, Noise, Timing, Capital — correspond exactly to the four evolutionary forces: Data↔V, Noise↔S, Timing↔I, Capital↔P. Open Trust is not a multiplier on the engine; it is the member-facing interface to it. **Derives from** A4+A5+A7+A8+A13+P5+T9+T18+T50+T51+T56. **Consequence:** any imbalance in the evolutionary forces is diagnosable directly from the disclosure gradient, and vice versa — the two objects are one system read from different sides. *Proof 18 in the constitution.*

## Duality & Convergence (T58–T60)

**T58 — Open-Trust ↔ INDX Duality.** The bijection between disclosure axes and V/S/I/P forces (T57) extends to the four INDX scoring dimensions — Originality, Performance, Persistence, Capacity — pairing Data↔Originality, Noise↔Performance, Timing↔Persistence, Capital↔Capacity. **Derives from** T57+A5+A7+T56+T9. **Consequence:** this closes the control loop — disclosure determines INDX score, which determines capital allocation, which shapes the next disclosure decision. *Proof 19 in the constitution.*

**T59 — Continuous Irreplaceability.** The historical 75/25 surplus split is a point on a continuous function of originality, not a flat tier — a flat tier would give providers of different originality identical shares, violating merit ($P6$) whenever originality genuinely differs. **Derives from** A9+T56+P6. **Consequence:** 75% is a reference point on the curve, not a ceiling or entitlement — actual share moves with actual originality. *Proof 20 in the constitution.*

**T60 — Control-Loop Convergence (Bellman Attractor).** Under standard rationality assumptions, the operator mapping a member's disclosure choice to their expected INDX-weighted allocation is a contraction on the disclosure space, and maximum disclosure is its unique fixed point. **Derives from** T58+T59+P6. **Consequence:** "the protocol evolves toward openness" is a proven convergence result, not a hope — observed drift away from the attractor is diagnosable as either risk-engine miscalibration or disclosure cost exceeding its return. *Proof 21 in the constitution.*

## Monetary Architecture (T61, T64)

**T61 — DWX Decay (Ownership Is Maintained, Not Granted).** DWX ownership decays every cycle without sustained contribution, mirroring the way an individual Quote decays without trading. **Derives from** A7+A9+P6+T16, self-similar with Quote decay. **Consequence:** this blocks the landed-gentry failure mode where early contributors accumulate ownership, stop contributing, and extract indefinitely — sustained merit becomes the continuous entry condition for ownership, not a one-time qualification.

**T64 — Patience Lever.** The internal matching rate rises monotonically with the length of the batching cycle, up to the point where delay cost — hedge drift, signal staleness — exceeds the marginal matching surplus gained. A longer window presents a larger cohort of opposing conviction to the same per-dimension matching capacity (A4), and a larger accumulated pool selects better (T18), which is what feeds $e^X$ (T5). This inverts the classical speed-as-edge thesis: for a meritocratic aggregator, patience — not microsecond speed — is the competitive advantage. **Derives from** A4+T18+T5. **Consequence:** the optimal cycle length is a measurable design parameter, set by where marginal netting surplus crosses marginal delay cost, not a race to zero latency.

## Custody & Settlement (T62–T63)

Both entries derive from A17, the v14 addition, and are the only two theorems that rest on it.

**T62 — Regulatory Surface.** The regulated surface, the attack surface and the balance-sheet surface all attach to custody and capital rather than to attestation: a protocol that certifies a money loop without holding the assets moving through it carries a materially smaller version of each. The claim is about where the surface concentrates, not about its disappearance — attestation is itself a regulated activity in some jurisdictions independently of custody, and rating agencies, benchmark administrators and auditors are the standing counterexample class. **Derives from** A17+A9+P6+T32. **Consequence:** the jurisdiction hedge is part of the theorem, not a caveat attached to it — T62 predicts a smaller and differently shaped surface, and is falsified by a regime in which the attestation function alone carries a surface comparable to custody's.

**T63 — Settlement.** The certified loop — allocation-in → fill → realised P&L → payout to the owner — is one cycle and is certified as one cycle, not as a set of independently attested legs. This is a conformance requirement on the matching and settlement components rather than a proposition the constitution assumes: an implementation SHALL certify the loop end to end, against a reference it does not control. **Derives from** A17+A2+P1+T42. **Consequence:** partial attestation is not a weaker version of the same guarantee — a loop certified leg by leg leaves the joins uncertified, which is exactly where an unattested step can be inserted.

**T63.1 — Settlement is not scoring.** Settling a cycle records what happened; scoring evaluates it. The two run on the same record but are separate operations, and the settlement path must not influence the estimate it exists to feed. **Derives from** T63+A2+A9. **Consequence:** a settlement component that could adjust a score would close a loop between the measurement and the thing measured, which is the failure A9 prohibits at the venue and A17 prohibits along the path.

**T63.2 — The fee is a leg.** The protocol's fee dissolves into the loop rather than sitting beside it: it is a leg of the certified cycle, taken inside it and recorded against the same reference everything else is certified against, not a charge levied alongside. **Derives from** T63+P6+A9+T44. **Consequence:** the fee is audited by the same mechanism that audits the allocation — there is no second accounting surface where a charge could sit unattested, which is what makes "the protocol follows the money" checkable rather than declared.
