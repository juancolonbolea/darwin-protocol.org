---
title: "Extraction (T18-T22)"
description: "How the framework identifies and closes extraction at the flow, stock, and governance levels."
---

Not all attrition or concentration is a problem — some of it is the filter doing its job. This group draws the line: it names the three levels extraction can hide at, and shows why all three, not just the most visible one, have to be closed.

---

**T18 — The 98% Are the Filtering Mechanism.** The large majority of participants who do not persist are not wasted effort. Their exit through the matching and selection process is the filter that concentrates trust on members who have genuine edge.

**Derives from** A4+T2. **Consequence:** attrition is not a metric to minimize at all costs — some of it is the mechanism working. The real design question is whether the filter is fair (A3), not whether exit is zero.

---

**T19 — Deadwood Shareholders Are Stock-Level Extraction.** A shareholder who holds equity but no longer contributes edge, flow, or development is extracting at the stock level, even when no flow-level rule (A9) is being broken.

**Derives from** A9+T15+A7. **Consequence:** closing extraction requires more than policing transaction-level rent-seeking — it requires a separate mechanism at the ownership-stock level, which T20 and T61 address.

---

**T20 — Three Extraction Levels Must All Be Closed.** Corruption resistance requires closing extraction at all three levels simultaneously: flow (A9), stock (T19), and governance (the power to block reinvestment). Closing any one alone leaves the others open as extraction channels.

**Derives from** A9+T19+T12. **Consequence:** a system airtight against flow-level extraction can still be extracted from at the stock or governance level — the constitution has to specify all three, not just the most visible one.

**Proof:** [Proof 2 in the constitution](/specification/proofs/proof-2-extraction).

---

**T21 — The Allocation Decision Is a Governance Surface.** The reinvestment cascade — rails and method, then exchange working capital, then sovereign capital — is a governance decision that determines the S-curve's trajectory (T16) and therefore the rate at which ownership converges with participation (T36). At full convergence, this governance belongs to members.

**Derives from** T17+T20. **Consequence:** who decides how surplus gets reinvested is not an administrative detail — it is the single lever that determines both how fast the protocol grows and how fast ownership returns to the people generating the trust.

---

**T22 — Mutualized Monopoly Is Structurally Aligned with Endgame.** A monopoly on internal matching that is mutualized (A9) rather than privately captured is structurally consistent with the endgame state where the community fully is the market (T3). Its size is the mechanism that gets the protocol there, not a competitive threat.

**Derives from** A9+T3. **Consequence:** scale is not in tension with the mission the way it usually is with a private monopoly. Because no private party can extract from the concentration, growth in matching density is unambiguously good.
