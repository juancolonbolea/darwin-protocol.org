---
title: "Inversion (T11-T14)"
description: "The credibility and power inversions, and why both are required before trust compounds."
---

Two axioms invert conventional market design: A13 sets the disclosure boundary so certified results are public instead of hidden, and A14 gives members governance standing and the residual claim on the venue. "At the table, not on the menu" is not axiom content — it is T12, derived below. T11–T14 show what each inversion does alone, and what happens once both operate together.

---

**T11 — Transparency Turns Selfishness into Social Trust.** When results are public, certified, and comparable (A13), a member acting in pure self-interest — trying to prove their own edge — generates a public good as a side effect: an attested, unfalsifiable track record the whole community can use to evaluate them.

**Derives from** A2+A7+A13. **Consequence:** the protocol does not need to appeal to altruism to produce trust. The self-interest that markets usually treat as a problem — hoarding information, exaggerating results — is redirected into a mechanism that produces public information instead. (Absorbs former T4, the same derivation applied specifically to INDX.)

---

**T12 — Power Inversion Organizes Individuals off the Menu.** Because no party — the operator included — may take the other side of member flow or trade on aggregate flow information (A9), and matching is mutualized (A4), individual intent is protected rather than exploited by a privileged counterparty. Members sit at the table as the mutualized market rather than on the menu as flow to be traded against. The netting mechanics that implement this are a conformance requirement on the matching and settlement components, not axiom content.

**Derives from** A4+A9+A14. **Consequence:** this is the structural reason the venue cannot become an adversary of its own members — there is no privileged internal position from which to trade against them.

---

**T13 — Both Inversions Together Create Compounding Trust.** The credibility inversion (T11) and the power inversion (T12) are not additive. Together they close both bridges of the trust equation — α (credibility → edge) and β (trust → confidence) — at once, producing compounding rather than one-off gains.

**Derives from** T11+T12. **Consequence:** a system with only one inversion resolves only one dimension of information asymmetry and stalls; both are required before the flywheel (T1) actually compounds.

---

**T14 — Competition Between Market Makers Is a Bug in Mutualized Systems.** In a system where no party may take the other side of member flow (A9) and matching is mutualized instead, a second, competing market maker inside the same venue is not healthy competition. It reintroduces exactly the privileged-counterparty position the power inversion (T12) removes.

**Derives from** A9+T12. **Consequence:** "more competition" is not a universal good inside this architecture. Inside the venue boundary, a second market maker directly undermines the power inversion rather than improving outcomes.
