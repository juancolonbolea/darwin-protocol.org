---
title: "S-Curve (T15-T17)"
description: "Why the protocol's growth curve is a designed output of the reinvestment rule, not an observed accident."
---

The S-curve group ties compensation timing and reinvestment allocation together. Front-loading, the shape of growth, and where surplus should be targeted are all the same underlying decision viewed at three different points.

---

**T15 — Front-Loading Is Compensation for Early Conviction.** Members and capital that commit before the flywheel (T1) has visibly engaged bear more risk than those who arrive after growth is proven. Front-loaded terms compensate for that asymmetry.

**Derives from** A5+T1. **Consequence:** early participation is priced compensation for conviction under uncertainty, not a gift — and its relative value should decline as the flywheel becomes self-evident to later entrants.

---

**T16 — The S-Curve Is Prescribed, Not Just Described.** Surplus is allocated across three reinvestment vectors — Compound, Source, Develop (A5) — with marginal rates that shift by phase: launch favors Source and Develop, growth shifts the mix as $e$ inflects, maturity settles into organic compounding at rate $e$. The resulting growth curve is what the allocation rule produces by construction, not an empirical pattern discovered after the fact.

**Derives from** A5+T15. **Consequence:** the shape of growth is a designed output of the allocation decision (T21) — which means the allocation decision, not the market, is where the shape of the future actually gets determined.

**Proof:** [Proof 3 in the constitution](/specification/proofs/proof-3-s-curve).

---

**T17 — "Wherever It Reduces P0 the Most" = Three-Vector Allocation.** Reinvestment surplus should target whichever information-asymmetry vector — credibility, intent, or data — is both largest and closest to the matching-density inflection where α-loop reinvestment begins to dominate external flow. Each vector that inflects funds the next.

**Derives from** A5+P0. **Consequence:** this gives the allocation rule (T16, T21) a concrete targeting criterion: not "invest evenly" but "invest where the marginal trust minted per unit of surplus is highest right now" — a criterion that changes shape as the S-curve progresses.
