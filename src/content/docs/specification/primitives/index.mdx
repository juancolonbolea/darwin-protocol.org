Primitives are the words the rest of the specification is written in. Each one is accepted without proof, and each one comes with an irreducibility test — a demonstration that it cannot be built out of the other primitives. Axioms are grounded in primitives. Theorems are derived from axioms. Nothing is grounded in nothing.

The canonical protocol documents (`spec/CONSTITUTION.md` §I, `spec/PROTOCOL.md` §5) define **seven primitives, P0 through P6**, ordered by causality. An eighth term, Inversion, was proposed as a primitive and then demoted — it is not a thing the protocol *has*, it is what the protocol *does*. It still gets a site page because it is load-bearing for understanding the other seven.

## P0 — Information Asymmetry

P0 has no page of its own in this site's navigation, because it is not one of the eight pages listed below — it is the problem the other primitives exist to resolve. The spec states it plainly: **the spread is the cost of one party knowing more than another.**

P0 splits into three irreducible dimensions, each resolved by a different primitive:

| Dimension | Question | Level | Resolved by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Credibility asymmetry | Is this merit real? | Individual | P1 (Credibility) |
| Intent asymmetry | What are others doing with my flow? | Community | P4 (Matching) |
| Data asymmetry | What information exists? | Society | P5 (Dataset) |

Trust (P3) is not something the protocol builds — it is the residual left over once all three asymmetries are removed. Geometrically, P0 is the non-overlap area of three circles, each a domain of information. As P0 falls, the circles converge toward concentricity: full information symmetry, zero friction.

## The causal chain

```
P0 (Information asymmetry)   Three dimensions: credibility, intent, data
        |
P1 (Credibility)              Certify edge -> Quote. Resolves credibility asymmetry.
        |
P2 (Confidence)                Capital allocated to certified credibility. Rational only
                                once P1 is standardised and comparable.
        |
P4 (Matching, by-product)      Resolves intent asymmetry. Effect, not goal.
        |
P5 (Dataset, open)             Resolves data asymmetry. Society-level commons.
        |
P3 (Trust = P1 x P2)            Residual of resolved asymmetry. Minted when
                                credibility meets confidence.
        |
P3 -> P1, P3 -> P2               Two feedback loops: more trust funds more data
                                and more proof, shrinking P0 further.
        |
P0 falls                       Surplus reinvested. e^X: growth compounds.
```

P6 (Merit) sits outside this chain as the ordering principle — the only primitive that is normative rather than descriptive. It does not get resolved by the chain; it judges the chain. See [P7 — Alignment](/specification/primitives/p7-alignment/) for the site page carrying P6.

**P6 is not the whole of the normative content.** P6 orders *reward*; it does not order *control*. Two commitments it does not yield are carried by axioms instead: [A9](/specification/axioms/structural/a9-no-extraction/) binds the venue against its own operator, and [A14](/specification/axioms/structural/a14-power-inversion/) gives members governance standing and the residual claim. The §0 amendment states this directly — P6 plus those two structural commitments — rather than widening P6 to cover control. Source: the axiom stress test, `analysis/axiom-stress/SYNTHESIS.md` in the repository.

## Site numbering vs. spec numbering

This site presents eight primitive pages, P1 through P8. The canonical spec numbers seven primitives, P0 through P6, plus a demoted P7. The two numbering schemes diverge — site P2 and P3 are swapped relative to the spec, for instance, because "Trust" reads better before "Confidence" in a member-facing narrative even though the spec derives Confidence first.

| Site page | Site label | Spec identity | Spec type |
|---|---|---|---|
| p1-edge | P1 — Edge | **P1 — Credibility (e)** | Stock (rate: $dC/dt$) |
| p2-trust | P2 — Trust | **P3 — Trust (INDX)** | Stock ($C \times K$) |
| p3-confidence | P3 — Confidence | **P2 — Confidence (K)** | Flow → Stock (AuM) |
| p4-matching | P4 — Matching | **P4 — Matching** | Process (by-product) |
| p5-selection | P5 — Selection | **P5 — The Dataset** | Stock asset |
| p6-transparency | P6 — Transparency | Grounded in A13 + P0's data dimension — not one of the spec's seven primitives | — |
| p7-alignment | P7 — Alignment | **P6 — Merit** | Ordering principle (normative) |
| p8-inversion | P8 — Inversion | ~~P7~~ — Inversion, demoted as a primitive, recognised as a protocol operation | Method, not primitive |

Every page below opens with a **Spec identity** line stating which canonical primitive it corresponds to, so the mapping is never left implicit.
