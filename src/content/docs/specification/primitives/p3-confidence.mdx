## Definition

Confidence is bestowed by allocating capital to certified credibility. It is forward-looking — a bet placed on observed P1 performance, not a settlement of past performance. Confidence is **given, not taken**: capital chooses to back a Quote, and once given, it must be honoured by the protocol's execution.

**Enabling condition.** Confidence is only rational when certified edge is expressed as a comparable instrument — risk-normalised at a uniform VaR standard, so a cautious trader and an aggressive trader are judged on skill, not leverage. Without that standardisation, comparability collapses and confidence is identically zero: $K = 0$.

## Type

Flow → Stock. Confidence begins as a flow (capital being allocated) and accumulates into a stock (AuM behind a given DARWIN).

## Verb & circle

**Honour.** Circle: **X** (the capital circle).

## Irreducibility test

Confidence cannot be derived from credibility alone. Credibility (P1) is proof of skill; it says nothing about whether anyone acts on it. Confidence requires two things credibility cannot supply by itself: an external act of trust, and an investable comparison surface that makes one Quote weighable against another. Skill without a market willing to back it stays a private fact, not confidence.

## Grounding

Confidence is the second link in the causal chain (P0 → P1 → P2 → P3): it resolves nothing about P0 directly, but it is the necessary bridge between credibility (individual-level) and trust (the product of both). The standardisation step that makes confidence rational is a structural axiom, not a primitive itself — it is what allows quality information to translate into allocation information.
