## Definition

Trust is not something the protocol constructs. It is the **residual of resolved information asymmetry** — what remains once credibility asymmetry, intent asymmetry, and data asymmetry have been removed. It is minted the instant certified credibility (P1) meets allocated confidence (P2). In protocol notation: $\text{INDX} = \Delta\text{Quote} \times \text{Outside Confidence}$ — new information created, multiplied by information acted upon. INDX is the measure of that impact.

## Type

Stock, defined as a product: $C \times K$ (credibility times confidence). Its rate of change follows the product rule: $\dfrac{dT}{dt} = \dfrac{dC}{dt} \cdot K + C \cdot \dfrac{dK}{dt}$.

## Verb & circle

**Accrue.** Circle: **e ∩ X** — the overlap between the edge circle and the capital circle. Trust lives exactly where credibility and confidence intersect, nowhere else.

## Irreducibility test

Trust cannot be derived from credibility or confidence in isolation. Credibility alone is unproven skill nobody has backed; confidence alone is capital with nothing certified to attach to. Trust requires the **product** of both — which is also why it cannot be manufactured directly. There is no action that mints trust without first producing edge and then having that edge backed with capital.

## Grounding

Trust is the terminal node of the primary causal chain (P0 → P1 → P2 → P3) and the source of the two feedback loops that make the system compound: trust reinvested into more data (α loop, P3 → P1) and trust reinvested into tighter proof (β loop, P3 → P2). Trust's velocity, $dT/dt$, is the rate at which information asymmetry is being resolved. Canonical treatment: `spec/CONSTITUTION.md` §I (row P3), `spec/VOCABULARY.md`.
