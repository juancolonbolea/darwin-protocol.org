## What is in the specification

The DARWIN Protocol is formally specified as:

- **7 irreducible primitives** (P0–P6) — the foundational terms that cannot be decomposed further; the site presents them under eight public labels, mapped on the [Primitives](/specification/primitives/) page
- **10 active axioms** (of A1–A16; three absorbed into others, three demoted) — the propositions the protocol assumes to be true (3 existential + 7 structural)
- **71 derived theorems** — 61 positive claims and 10 failure theorems that follow logically from the axioms, plus one lemma, **L1 (Matching Density)**, which carries the scaling in membership that A4 implies without asserting
- **21 formal proofs** — written derivations of the load-bearing results
- **2 empirical anchors** — the two places the specification reaches outside itself: **E1** (T42, external validation — the falsifiability condition) and **E2** (A1, access dominance — the motivating condition), each with a stated measurement and falsifier

The axiom set is at v13, the output of an adversarial audit of every axiom — one derivation attempt each, then refutation and defence — recorded in `analysis/axiom-stress/SYNTHESIS.md` in the repository. Thirteen active axioms were reduced to ten: A10 became vocabulary, and A15 and A16 became conformance requirements on the matching and settlement components. The same revision added L1, the anchor E2, and a tenth failure theorem — **F10 (Surplus Not Retained)**, what happens when rescued surplus is rebated or held idle instead of redeployed.

The target is for the full logical chain to be compiler-verified in **Lean 4** — a proof assistant used in academic mathematics and formal verification. If an axiom changes, dependent theorems break, and the compiler catches it. The Lean formalisation is an open community component; see [Lean 4 Formalization](/specification/lean4/) for status.

## Why formal specification matters

Most financial products are described in marketing documents and legal disclaimers. The claims are vague. The reasoning is hidden. The customer is asked to trust the institution.

The DARWIN Protocol takes the opposite approach. Every claim is derived from stated axioms. Every derivation is verified by a compiler. Every assumption is public.

This means:

- **You can verify the claims yourself.** The axioms are stated. The derivations are shown.
- **Changes are traceable.** If an axiom is modified, every dependent theorem is automatically re-evaluated.
- **Contradictions are caught.** Once the Lean 4 formalisation lands, the compiler enforces logical consistency across the entire specification.

## How to navigate

Use the sidebar to explore the hierarchy:

### Primitives
The irreducible terms that form the vocabulary of the protocol. Each primitive has an irreducibility test — a demonstration that it cannot be expressed in terms of the other primitives.

- Edge, Trust, Confidence, Matching, Selection, Transparency, Alignment, Inversion

### Axioms
The foundational propositions, organized into two groups:

- **Existential axioms (A1-A3)** — Why the protocol must exist. The market failures it addresses.
- **Structural axioms (A4-A14)** — How the protocol operates. The rules it follows.

### Theorems
71 derived claims plus the lemma L1, grouped by theme:

- **Core Flywheel (L1, T1-T4)** — The density lemma and the basic growth engine
- **Exponential (T5-T10)** — Why growth compounds
- **Inversion (T11-T14)** — How power shifts from institutions to members
- **S-Curve (T15-T17)** — The lifecycle dynamics
- **Extraction (T18-T22)** — Why value capture is impossible
- **Structural (T23-T28)** — Governance and long-term stability
- **Extended (T29-T64)** — Transition, certification, information architecture, monetary architecture
- **Failure Theorems (F1-F10)** — What breaks the flywheel, and the four Named Principles

### Proofs
21 written proofs, plus the two empirical anchors (E1 and E2) that carry the framework's contact with reality. Six proofs are rendered as pages — the exponential growth proof, the extraction impossibility proof, the S-curve, the community-beats-market proof, the dataset vehicle and the linear-network decomposition; the rest are indexed on the Proofs overview.

### Lean 4 Formalization
The specification expressed in a language a machine can check for consistency. Open community component — scope, status and acceptance criteria.

### Cross-Validation Matrix
A complete mapping of which axioms support which theorems, and which proofs validate which claims.
