## What is in the specification

The DARWIN Protocol is formally specified as:

- **8 irreducible primitives** — the foundational terms that cannot be decomposed further
- **13 foundational axioms** — the propositions the protocol assumes to be true (3 existential + 10 structural)
- **28 derived theorems** — claims that follow logically from the axioms
- **6 formal proofs** — rigorous derivations of key results

The full logical chain is compiler-verified in **Lean 4** — a mathematical proof assistant used in academic mathematics and formal verification research. If an axiom changes, dependent theorems break, and the compiler catches it.

No financial protocol in the world offers this level of formal verification.

## Why formal specification matters

Most financial products are described in marketing documents and legal disclaimers. The claims are vague. The reasoning is hidden. The customer is asked to trust the institution.

The DARWIN Protocol takes the opposite approach. Every claim is derived from stated axioms. Every derivation is verified by a compiler. Every assumption is public.

This means:

- **You can verify the claims yourself.** The axioms are stated. The derivations are shown. The Lean 4 code is available.
- **Changes are traceable.** If an axiom is modified, every dependent theorem is automatically re-evaluated.
- **Contradictions are impossible.** The compiler ensures logical consistency across the entire specification.

## How to navigate

Use the sidebar to explore the hierarchy:

### Primitives
The 8 irreducible terms that form the vocabulary of the protocol. Each primitive has an irreducibility test — a demonstration that it cannot be expressed in terms of the other primitives.

- Edge, Trust, Confidence, Matching, Selection, Transparency, Alignment, Inversion

### Axioms
13 foundational propositions, organized into two groups:

- **Existential axioms (A1-A3)** — Why the protocol must exist. The market failures it addresses.
- **Structural axioms (A4-A14)** — How the protocol operates. The rules it follows.

### Theorems
28 derived claims, grouped by theme:

- **Core Flywheel (T1-T4)** — The basic growth engine
- **Exponential (T5-T10)** — Why growth compounds
- **Inversion (T11-T14)** — How power shifts from institutions to members
- **S-Curve (T15-T17)** — The lifecycle dynamics
- **Extraction (T18-T22)** — Why value capture is impossible
- **Structural (T23-T28)** — Governance and long-term stability

### Proofs
6 formal proofs of key results, including the exponential growth proof, the extraction impossibility proof, and the community-beats-market proof.

### Lean 4 Formalization
The compiler-verified source code. The specification expressed in a language that a machine can check for consistency.

### Cross-Validation Matrix
A complete mapping of which axioms support which theorems, and which proofs validate which claims.
